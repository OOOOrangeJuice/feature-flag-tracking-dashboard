Feature flag cleanup map

A top-to-bottom view of how 3,541 ECS configurations narrow into 314 remaining 1JS cleanup flags and their next actions.

How to read this map. Read it from top to bottom. Each card shows a count and what it represents, and every card is a subset of the card above it.

Lines. A solid grey line is an exact split, so the children add up to the parent. A dashed line links a reconciliation note that is not part of the split. Where two lines meet in one card, that card is a merge: two cards from one branch combine into a new parent, which starts the next branch. Select any card to highlight its full path.

Layout. Cards that keep splitting stay in the left column. Cards that stop splitting sit to their right on the same row. Click any card to highlight its full path, and hover over or click the small "!" next to a card title to see what that group means.

Units. The top card counts ECS configurations, which are not 1:1 with 1JS flags, because one configuration can hold several flights. Lower cards count flags.

Snapshots. The counts from 2,256 down to 1,492 come from the August snapshot taken at the start of the cleanup cycle. They were captured on different days, so 1,781 + 466 is 9 short of 2,256.

Active vs. stale rollouts. Both are incomplete ECS rollouts. An active rollout had an ECS update in the last four months and is still in development. A stale rollout has not been updated for more than four months, so its owner must decide whether to abandon it, roll it out, or leave it alone.
